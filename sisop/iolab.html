I/O Lab

Processes and threads make real system calls: reading and writing the disk, receiving and sending on the network, reading the keyboard and writing to the screen. Each device keeps its own queue and serves it with its own policy. Watch who waits, where, and why.

Network

1 ms to receive a packet · 1 ms per 4 KB to send
Socket buffer
Incoming packets are served first, then outgoing ones, each in arrival order. A packet goes to the thread waiting on its port, or into that port's socket buffer. If the buffer is full it is dropped, and the sender resends it 20 ms later.
Card queue

Keyboard

one interrupt per key · echoed to the screen
Terminal mode
Only the foreground process may read the keyboard. A background process that calls read() is stopped with SIGTTIN, all of its threads, until it is brought to the foreground. Inside the foreground process, waiting threads are served first come, first served.

Display

a slow terminal · prints 1 character per ms
write() copies text into the output queue (24 characters) and returns. When the queue is full the writer blocks, and wakes once it drains to 16. Keyboard echo goes into the same queue.
